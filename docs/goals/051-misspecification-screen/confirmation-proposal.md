## Proposed complete-workload ceiling

Use the same deep-server-ts resource limits: **eight workers, numerical threads
one, 32 GiB address space**. Request **4,500 elapsed computation seconds
(75 minutes)** for the whole tranche. Charge preparation/controls as 600 s or
their actual computation if greater; reserve at least 900 s for synthesis,
verification and independent result review. Allow timing at most 600 s and
include 30 s termination grace in the budget.

The completed 17-cell production required 1,301 s. As a deliberately
conservative starting allowance, 1.5 times that entire workload is 1,952 s,
without assuming a proportional speed-up from dropping fifteen scored cells.
Together with 600 s preparation, 600 s timing, 900 s synthesis/verification and
30 s grace, this is **4,082 s (68.0 minutes)**, below the proposed cap. Two-cell
production should be cheaper, but this is an estimate, not qualification.
The new complete-cell timing must measure integration, field generation,
scoring, serialization and verification; only its passing bound projection
can authorize a production launch. Count one-time integration once. Stop if
the full workload does not fit; do not enlarge resources or replication.

## Reporting horizon and decision

The requested authority would cover two-cell implementation, focused controls,
independent implementation review, source-bound timing, one production block,
fixed synthesis, inspected evidence, independent result review and the final
scientific report/Correspondence update. Preserve a negative or unresolved
confirmation as a valid outcome. No commit, push or publication is included.

A confirmed result would justify developing a separate Stage 2 comparison
around a fitted background mechanism and the neutral comparator. Its model
formulation, nuisance treatment, identifiability and predictive evaluation
remain scientific design choices for a later proposal. This confirmation
does not authorize those fits, more landscapes, unknown-nuisance experiments,
real mink data, or broader claims about observational adequacy.
